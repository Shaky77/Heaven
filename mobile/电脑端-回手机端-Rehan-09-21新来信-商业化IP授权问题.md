But if you ever pull the actual engine source into something you're selling, AGPL attaches: the source has to stay open, and if it's offered over a network or as an API, §13 requires you to make the source available to the people using it. Your own implementation, your own licence — no problem. Our code folded into a closed product — that would not work.

**Attribution — keep doing what you already do.** You've carried "theory: Shaky77 / implementation: Rehan" from the start, and that's exactly right. Keep it visible in whatever commercial form this takes. One thing I'd add: make sure it doesn't read as the official Weiwen's Law product, or as endorsed by me. "Independent implementation of Weiwen's Law, by Rehan" is accurate, and it's the strongest version of it — it's true, and it keeps your work yours and mine mine. That framing protects us both: your product shouldn't be mistaken for something I ship or support, and I shouldn't be attached to a product I don't control.

For the record, I'm not a lawyer — treat the above as my understanding of my own licensing, not legal advice. If you're at the point of formal steps, it's worth having someone qualified look at the AGPL §13 question specifically, if any of our code ends up in scope.

And to be explicit, since you asked about alignment: I'm not looking for a fee, a licence, or a share. You built it, you own it. If it turns into something real in production, that's the best outcome I could want for the framework — more than anything it would be evidence the structure holds up outside my own stack.

Good luck on the business side. Send the trace whenever it's ready.

Best, Shaky
